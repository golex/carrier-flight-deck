Flight Deck

A schematic of one cyclic-operations cycle on a Nimitz-class aircraft carrier: twelve jets launched, twelve recovered, and the deck respotted for the next event. This page says what is drawn from documents and what is an estimate.

What it shows

The ship is drawn to scale, 1,092 feet long and 252 feet across the flight deck, with four steam catapults, four arresting wires, four deck-edge elevators and the island on the starboard side. The landing area is angled nine degrees to port, which is why the two waist catapults have to be secured before anything can land. The jets are F/A-18E and F/A-18F Super Hornets and EA-18G Growlers only.

The sequence follows a day, visual-conditions cycle on a ninety-minute schedule: man up and start, a twelve-jet launch off all four catapults, the waist secured and the deck turned green, a twelve-trap recovery with one bolter and one foul-deck wave-off, then the respot. Everything is precomputed, so the playback can be scrubbed and the speed changed without the choreography drifting.

Reading the deck

Every dot is a person. The jersey colour says what they are allowed to do, and only a yellow shirt may signal a pilot to move.

JerseyWho
YellowAircraft directors, catapult officers (the shooters), the aircraft handling officer and the Air Boss.
GreenCatapult and arresting gear crews, hook runners, and the squadrons' maintenance troubleshooters.
BluePlane handlers: chocks and chains, tractor drivers, elevator operators.
RedOrdnance, and crash and salvage.
BrownPlane captains, one per jet.
PurpleAviation fuels, the grapes.
WhiteSafety observers, final checkers, landing signal officers, medical.

Documented or estimated

The research behind this page separates every number into documented, measured, single-source and estimated. The following are documented in Navy manuals and official releases.

  • Deck dimensions, the nine-degree angle, the 310-foot catapult stroke, the two-second stroke to about 150 knots, and the Mk 7 arresting gear rated to stop a 50,000-pound jet in 344 feet.
  • Jersey colours and roles, the hand signals, and the rule that one director controls one jet at a time.
  • The launch sequence (holdback, launch bar, tension, military power, the salute, the shooter's touch of the deck, the deck-edge operator pressing fire) and the suspend procedure.
  • The recovery procedure: clear deck forward, the deck status light, the hook runner, the wire retract, and the half-mile foul-deck wave-off rule.
  • The cyclic-operations pattern: launch before land, the waist locked out during recovery, the crash crane running throughout, and the Case I overhead pattern altitudes and distances.

The following are estimates, chosen to satisfy the documented rules and tuned by eye. They should not be quoted as fact.

  • The positions of the island, elevators, catapults, wires and lens were measured from public overhead imagery of a sister ship to within a few feet; the foul-line offset, the parking slots and their nicknames, and every crew standing position in feet are reconstructions.
  • Step durations inside the launch and recovery, the same-catapult interval, taxi and tow speeds and the wing-fold time are calibrated to published averages (a twelve-jet launch in eight to ten minutes, a ramp interval of 45 to 60 seconds) rather than timed from video.
  • The number of people on deck is reduced for legibility. A real cycle has 150 to 250 people topside.
  • Squadron numbers and modexes are illustrative.
