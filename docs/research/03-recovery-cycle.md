# 03 - The Arrested Recovery Cycle (Landing)

Scope: Nimitz-class CVN, jet aircraft only (F/A-18E/F Super Hornet "Rhino", EA-18G Growler).
Confidence key: **[C]** = confirmed in 2+ sources; **[S]** = single source; **[E]** = engineering estimate / composite for simulation (not a published figure).

---

## 1. Recovery "Cases" - which one and why

| Case | When used | Weather minima | Control | Spacing at ramp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Case I** | Day, VMC expected throughout | Ceiling >= 3,000 ft and visibility >= 5 nm in the carrier control zone **[C]** | Visual, "zip lip" (near radio silence); aircraft hold overhead in the port holding pattern and self-sequence | ~45-60 s **[C]** |
| **Case II** | Day, IMC possible during descent | Ceiling >= 1,000 ft and visibility >= 5 nm **[C]** | Hybrid: Case III radar-controlled descent until inside ~10 nm with "ship in sight" ("see you at 10"), then join a Case I-style visual pattern **[C]** | ~45-60 s |
| **Case III** | Night (from 30 min before sunset to 30 min after sunrise) **or** weather below Case II minima **[C]** | Below 1,000 ft / 5 nm, or any night ops | Full radar control from CATCC (Carrier Air Traffic Control Center); single-aircraft approaches; precise marshal + push times | ~60 s ("exactly one minute behind each other") **[C]** |

Airspace: Carrier Control Area (CCA) = 50 nm radius, surface upward, controlled by CATCC. Carrier Control Zone = 5 nm radius, surface to 2,500 ft **[C]**.

## 2. Case I overhead pattern - step by step

All turns are **left-hand** (to port). The ship steams into the wind at the Base Recovery Course (BRC); the landing area is angled ~9-10 deg to port of the ship's centerline, so the final bearing is ~9-10 deg left of ship heading **[C]**.

### 2.1 The "stack" (port holding pattern / "overhead")
- Left-hand circle, roughly tangent to the BRC, with the ship at the aircraft's 3 o'clock; max diameter 5 nm **[C]**.
- Lowest altitude 2,000 ft; additional levels every 1,000 ft (2,000 / 3,000 / 4,000 ... ) **[C]**.
- Typically two squadrons/sections per altitude, on opposite sides of the circle **[S, Fighter Sweep]**.
- When the deck opens, the 2,000 ft group descends out to the initial; everyone above **spirals down one level** **[C]**.
- The low group watches the launch: they time their arrival so the first jet ("breaking the deck") is in the groove just as the last launcher is off the catapult and the landing area is clear. Too early = foul-deck wave-off / "spin"; too late = wasted deck time **[S, Fighter Sweep]**.

### 2.2 The Initial and the Break
- **Initial**: 3 nm astern, 800 ft, paralleling BRC (slightly to starboard of the ship), timed to the Expected/Charlie time **[C]**.
- Flights (2-4 ship) arrive in echelon, typically ~300-350 KIAS **[C/S: 300 kt in CNATRA T-45 guidance; up to 350 kt in fleet/sim descriptions]**.
- **The Break**: a level, hard (~4-6 g **[E]**) 180 deg left turn at **800 ft**, normally over the ship or up to ~1-2 nm ahead of the bow, within 3 nm of the ship **[C for 800 ft; break point E]**.
- **Break interval** within a flight ~15-20 s per aircraft (CNATRA: 15 s, wings-level hook-down 20 s; sim/fleet descriptions ~17 s) **[S]**. Pilots then fine-tune downwind extension/turn point so ramp spacing ends up ~45-60 s.
- During the break: throttles idle, speedbrake, decelerate below gear speed; **gear down, flaps full, hook down**; slow to on-speed AOA (indexer "donut").
- **Spin pattern**: if >6 aircraft are in the landing pattern or spacing is bad, a jet climbs to 1,200 ft and flies a tight 360 within 3 nm, then re-enters **[C]**.

### 2.3 Downwind and the "Abeam" / "180"
- Descend to **600 ft** on downwind **[C]**.
- Abeam distance from the ship: **~1.1-1.3 nm** **[C]** (CNATRA T-45: 1.0-1.1 nm).
- "The 180": the point abeam the LSO platform / landing area where the final turn starts; position is adjusted to give a **15-18 s groove** **[C]**.
- Abeam: pilot completes landing checklist ("abeam call" - gear, flaps, hook, in Case I usually not voiced) **[S]**.

### 2.4 The "90"
- Halfway through the descending left turn: **~450 ft, ~1.2 nm (some describe ~0.9-1.0 nm) behind/left of the ship, 90 deg of turn to go** **[C]**.

### 2.5 Crossing the wake / rolling into "the groove"
- Cross the wake at ~**370 ft**; pilot acquires the "ball" on the IFLOLS **[C]**.
- **The groove**: wings-level final along the angled-deck centerline. Target **15-18 s** (LSO groove timer; >18 s "long groove", <15 s "short/overshooting") **[C]**.
- At ~120 kt closure (approach ~135-145 KIAS minus ~25-30 kt wind over deck), 18 s is ~0.6-0.75 nm of track **[E]**.
- Glideslope typically **3.5 deg** (lens may be set ~3.25-4.0 deg depending on wind over deck) **[E/common knowledge, not found in fetched sources]**.
- Touchdown sink rate ~700-800 ft/min; no flare **[C]**.
- Pilot makes on the order of 200-300 small corrections in the ~18 s groove **[S, AOPA]**.

### 2.6 The ball call
- Made as the jet rolls wings-level with the ball in sight (~3/4 nm). Format: **side number, type, "ball", fuel state (thousands of lb)** **[C]**.
  - Example: **"Three-oh-six, Rhino ball, six-point-five."** (Growler: "Growler ball"; legacy Hornet: "Hornet ball").
- LSO answers **"Roger ball"** (= cleared to continue/land) **[C]**.
- "**Clara**" = pilot does not see the ball; LSO talks him down **[C]**.
- In zip-lip/EMCON/NORDO, the LSO flashes the green cut lights in place of "Roger ball" **[C]**.

### 2.7 Touchdown and arrestment
- Aim point: between the 2 and 3 wire; **target the #3 wire** on a 4-wire ship (2-wire on 3-wire ships: CVN-76 Reagan, CVN-77 Bush) **[C]**.
- On touchdown the pilot **advances throttles to MIL (full military power)** and holds it ~3 s (in case of bolter), then retards to idle once stopped **[C]**.
- Arrestment: stops a ~50,000 lb aircraft at 130 kt in **~344 ft (105 m) within ~2 s** (Mk 7 Mod 3 rated runout; ideal stop ~340 ft) **[C]**. Real F/A-18 traps roll out ~250-340 ft **[E]**.
- Wire tension then pulls the jet **back** a few feet after it stops (rollback) - pilot allows it so the hook can drop the wire **[E/common]**.

### 2.8 Bolter and wave-off
- **Bolter**: hook misses all wires (or skips). Because power is already at MIL, the jet simply flies off the angled deck. Climb straight ahead to **1,200 ft**, then into the bolter/wave-off pattern (upwind, left turn to downwind, re-enter at 600 ft) **[C]**.
- **Wave-off**: ordered by the LSO by radio ("Wave off, wave off!") and/or the flashing red **wave-off lights** on the lens (mandatory). Causes: unsafe pass (technique wave-off) or **foul deck** ("Wave off, foul deck") **[C]**.
- Clear-deck cutoff: if the deck isn't clear by roughly 100 ft / ~8 s before touchdown, the LSO waves the jet off **[S, forum]**.
- A bolter/wave-off adds roughly 2.5-4 min before that jet is back in the groove **[E]**.

## 3. IFLOLS ("the lens", "the meatball")
- Location: port side of the landing area, abeam the wire set (outboard, forward of the LSO platform) **[C]**.
- **Ball**: amber/yellow light from a Fresnel lens cell stack; on glidepath = level with the **green datum lights** (horizontal row each side). High = ball above datum; low = below; dangerously low = **red** ball; > ~3/4 deg high it goes off the top **[C]**.
- IFLOLS: 12 cells (5 FLOLS + 7), vertical coverage ~1.7 deg, usable to ~1.5 nm **[S]**.
- **Wave-off lights**: red flashing lamps either side; when lit all other lamps extinguish; mandatory go-around **[C]**.
- **Cut lights**: row of (4) green lights above the lens; "Roger ball"/clear to land in zip-lip, then "add power" flashes **[C]**.
- Stabilized against ship pitch/roll (and heave in inertial mode); roll angle set per aircraft type for hook-to-eye distance **[S]**.

## 4. LSO ("Paddles") team and the platform
- Platform: **port side, aft**, at deck edge near the stern behind/aft of the port elevator (elevator 4); has wind screen and an escape net/pit **[C]**.
- Equipment: radio handset, **"the pickle"** (hand-held switch controlling wave-off and cut lights) **[C]**, deck status (clear/foul) light, wind/glideslope displays, PLAT camera monitor.
- Team (each squadron supplies 3-5 LSOs; Air Wing LSO "CAG Paddles" in charge) **[C]**:
  - **Controlling LSO** - holds the pickle, talks glideslope/AOA, assigns the grade.
  - **Backup LSO** - watches lineup, confirms landing area clear, assists grading.
  - **Deck caller / deck-status LSO** - faces forward, calls "clear deck"/"foul deck", watches for people/FOD.
  - **Groove timer** (15-18 s) and **interval timer** (time since last trap) - reported as e.g. "49 over 16" = 49 s interval, 16 s groove **[S, Fighter Sweep]**.
  - **Writer** - records each pass in LSO shorthand.
- Typical voice calls: "Power", "Right for lineup", "You're high", "Easy with it", "Wave off, foul deck", "WAVE OFF!" **[C]**.

### LSO grades (Greenie Board)
| Grade | Points | Meaning | Board color |
|---|---|---|---|
| OK (underlined) | 5 | Perfect pass, usually in bad conditions; rare | green (special) |
| OK | 4 | Reasonable deviations with good corrections | green |
| (OK) "Fair" | 3 | Deviations with adequate corrections | yellow |
| Bolter | 2.5 | Hook missed all wires | blue/other |
| No Grade | 2 | Below average but safe | brown |
| Technique wave-off | 1 | Unsafe pass waved off | red-ish |
| Cut | 0 | Unsafe; gross deviations inside wave-off window | red |
| Foul-deck wave-off | - | Not counted | - |
Sources agree on all except No Grade (2.0 most common; one source says 2.5) **[C]**.

## 5. Arresting gear and the crews

### Hardware
- **4 cross-deck pendants** (wires) on most Nimitz CVNs (CVN-76/77 have 3) **[C]**. Spacing ~**40-50 ft** apart **[C: ~50 ft cited]**; #1 wire is nearest the ramp (~150-200 ft forward of the round-down **[E]**).
- Pendant: 1-1/4 to 1-3/8 in. wire rope, held several inches above deck by curved leaf-spring supports; connected at each end to a **purchase cable** running to its own **Mk 7 Mod 3 hydro-pneumatic arresting engine** (~43 tons each) in engine rooms below the flight deck **[C]**.
- Pendants replaced every **~125 arrestments**; pendant change takes ~2-3 min **[S]**.
- **Constant Runout Control Valve (CROV)**: engine set for each aircraft's weight so every trap uses the same runout. Over-light setting -> "two-blocking" (excessive runout) **[C]**.

### Weight setting / Aircraft Recovery Bulletin
- **Aircraft Recovery Bulletins (ARBs)** (NAVAIR) specify authorized weights, engaging speeds and engine settings per aircraft type **[C]**.
- Sequence: pilot's fuel state -> Pri-Fly/Air Boss & arresting gear officer determine landing weight -> each **engine room operator** sets the weight selector; setting repeated to Pri-Fly and the deck-edge station (synchro readouts) and verified before "clear deck" **[C]**. In normal ops a single setting (max trap weight for the type) is often used **[S, Wikipedia]**.

### People (green shirts, V-2 Division; Arresting Gear Officer in yellow)
- **Arresting Gear Officer (AGO)** (yellow) - at the landing area, cannot give "clear deck" unless all sheaves are up and engines set; interlocked with the clear/foul deck light **[C]**.
- **Deck-edge operator** (green, in the port catwalk) - operates the retract control after each arrestment **[C]**.
- **Engine room operators** (green, below deck; one per engine) - set weight, monitor engine, retract/battery position **[C]**.
- **Hook runner(s)** (green) - run out to the jet after it stops; ensure the pendant/purchase cable is off the hook (pull it off by hand if needed, ducking under hot tailpipes); then give the **retract** signal to the deck-edge operator; can show an emergency hold signal **[C]**.
- **Gear puller / landing-area director** (yellow) - immediately after the stop, signals the pilot "hook up" (or pull cable free) and then hands the jet off to the next director to taxi clear **[C]**.
- Others visible in landing area: crash & salvage (red), safety observers (white), LSOs (white), VLA electricians (green) **[C]**.
- The system can be reset in as little as **~35 s** after an arrestment **[S, Smithsonian]**.

## 6. After the trap - clearing the landing area ("foul deck" -> "clear deck")
1. **t = 0 s** hook engages; **t ~2 s** jet stopped; pilot at MIL then idle.
2. **t ~3-5 s** rollback; gear puller (yellow) signals **hook up**; pilot raises hook, wire drops.
3. **t ~5-8 s** hook runner (green) verifies hook clear, pendant on deck; signals retract.
4. **t ~6-15 s** pendant retracted by engines pulling purchase cable back; pendant re-tensioned on its supports **[E for timings]**.
5. Directors (yellow) taxi the jet **forward and right out of the landing area** across the foul line toward the starboard bow/"the street", pilot **folds wings while taxiing** **[C]**; directors hand off jet-to-jet ("passing" the aircraft).
6. Once the jet's tail is clear of the foul line and all wires are reset and personnel clear, the AGO gives **clear deck**; deck status light goes from red (foul) to green (clear); LSO deck caller confirms "**Clear deck!**" **[C]**.
7. Next jet must be at ~100 ft or higher when clear deck is given or it is waved off.
- Net: the landing area is foul for ~20-35 s of each ~45-60 s interval **[E]**.

Where they go: during recovery the angled landing area must stay clear, so recovering jets park forward - on the **bow** (cat 1/2 area) and along the starboard side forward of the island; after the last recovery, aircraft are stored on the bow **[C]**. Between events the deck is **respotted** (towed by blue-shirt tractors / taxied) to set up the next launch **[C]**.

## 7. Case III (night / IFR) recovery

- **Marshal**: CATCC assigns each jet a marshal radial (typically ~150 deg relative from BRC, i.e. behind the ship), DME, altitude, and an **Expected Approach Time (EAT / push time)** **[C]**.
  - Rule of thumb: **DME = altitude (thousands) + 15**; lowest = 6,000 ft at 21 nm; 1,000 ft / 1 nm separation upward (7,000 ft at 22 nm, ...) **[C, sim/training sources]**.
  - Holding: left-hand **6-minute racetrack** (1-min legs, 2-min turns). Push within **+/- 10 s** of EAT **[C]**.
  - Push times separated by **1 minute** **[C]**.
- **Descent**: 250 kt, 4,000 ft/min to the **"Platform" at 5,000 ft**, then 2,000 ft/min **[C]**.
- **Arc** at 12 nm (12.5 nm) if marshal radial is >10 deg off final bearing **[C]**.
- **10 nm**: dirty up (gear/flaps/hook), slow to on-speed, 1,200 ft **[C]**.
- **6 nm fix**: 1,200 ft, ~150 kt; begin intercept of glideslope ~3 nm **[C]**.
- Glidepath checkpoints: 1,200 ft at 3 nm, 860 ft at 2 nm, 460 ft at 1 nm, ~360 ft at the ball call (~3/4 nm) **[S, Wikipedia]**.
- Approach guidance: CCA controller talk-down, ICLS "bullseye", **ACLS** (Mode I = coupled to touchdown; Mode IA = coupled to 3/4 nm; Mode II = needles only), long-range laser lineup system **[C]**.
- At **3/4 nm**: ball call; LSO takes control visually. Bolters/wave-offs climb to 1,200 ft, go to the bolter pattern and get radar vectors back ("tanker" if low fuel - **bingo** shown by alternating wave-off and cut lights) **[C]**.
- Result: one jet roughly every **60 s** - slower than Case I **[C]**.

## 8. Timing of a whole recovery and the cycle

- Typical event: **12-20 aircraft** **[C]**.
- Case I: 45-60 s interval -> 16 jets ~ **12-16 min** plus bolters/wave-offs; a full recovery is usually **~15-20 min** **[E from C intervals]**.
- Case III: ~60 s -> 16 jets ~ **16-20 min** + bolters; often 20-30 min **[E]**.
- **Cyclic ops**: cycles ~1 h 30 min typical (range 1 h - 1 h 45 min) **[C]**. Order in each cycle: **launch Event N+1 first** (~15 min), *then* immediately recover Event N, then respot / fuel / arm for the next launch **[C]**.
- The recovery cannot begin until the last launcher is off - the landing area is used as parking/taxi area during launch (aircraft on the waist cats 3/4 foul the angled deck) **[C]**.
- "Ready deck" for next launch: after the last trap, jets for the next event are fueled, armed and respotted; first launch of next event is ~1 h after previous launch start **[S]**.
- Interval between "last trap" and "first launch of next cycle" ~ 45-60 min **[E]**.

## 9. Simulation implications

What to model (with suggested numbers):

1. **Case selector** (I / II / III) driving: pattern geometry (overhead vs. straight-in), interval (50 s vs 60 s), radio-silence vs. CATCC callouts, day/night lighting.
2. **Holding stack (Case I)**: left-hand 5 nm circle at 2,000/3,000/4,000 ft, ship at 3 o'clock; groups descend one level as the low group departs. In a top-down deck view this is off-screen; show in a zoomed-out "overhead" view with altitude labels.
3. **Pattern path (Case I)** as a spline: initial (3 nm astern, 800 ft, ~350 kt) -> break (level left 180 deg at 800 ft) -> downwind (600 ft, 1.1-1.3 nm abeam, ~150 kt) -> 180 -> 90 (450 ft) -> wake crossing (370 ft) -> groove (15-18 s on the ~9 deg angled centerline) -> touchdown. Break spacing ~15-20 s within a flight; adjust downwind length so ramp interval = 45-60 s.
4. **Ball call text overlay**: "306, Rhino ball, 6.5" / "Roger ball"; occasional "Clara", "Power", "Right for lineup".
5. **IFLOLS indicator widget**: ball vs. datum lights, red wave-off flash, green cut lights.
6. **LSO platform** on the port quarter with 4-6 white-shirt figures; groove timer/interval timer HUD ("49 over 16").
7. **Touchdown**: hook catches wire 1-4 with probabilities (e.g. #3 ~50%, #2 ~25%, #4 ~15%, #1 ~5%, bolter ~5-8%); throttle-to-MIL exhaust effect on touchdown.
8. **Rollout**: decelerate from ~120 kt ground speed to 0 over ~250-340 ft in ~2 s (strong ease-out), then ~5 ft rollback.
9. **Bolter**: jet continues off the angled deck, climbs out, reappears in pattern ~3 min later. **Wave-off** (foul deck or technique): flashing red lens lights, jet passes over the deck without touching.
10. **Deck state machine**: CLEAR -> (trap) FOUL -> hook up -> hook runner check -> retract -> jet past foul line -> engines set -> CLEAR. Display a clear/foul indicator; if a jet reaches ~8 s from touchdown while FOUL -> foul-deck wave-off.
11. **Green-shirt crew**: hook runner sprinting to the jet tail, deck-edge operator in port catwalk, pendant animating back to its tensioned position. Yellow-shirt gear puller giving "hook up", then a chain of directors taxiing the jet forward/right while **wings fold** (~15-20 s fold animation).
12. **Parking flow**: landed jets go forward to bow and starboard side forward of island ("the street"/"the row"); landing area always kept clear; red crash crew and white safety observers stationed by the landing area.
13. **Weight setting step**: before each trap, show engine-room/AGO setting weight (e.g. "Rhino 44,000" ) from the ARB - a nice "hidden complexity" callout.
14. **Case III mode**: single jets on a straight-in final from 10 nm at 1 min spacing, marshal stack labels (6,000 ft/21 nm, 7,000 ft/22 nm...), red deck lighting, ACLS/needles callout.
15. **Cycle timeline**: launch Event N+1 (~15 min) -> recover Event N (12-20 jets, ~15-20 min) -> respot (~30-45 min) -> repeat on a ~90 min cycle; allow time-acceleration.
16. **Scores**: optional LSO grade per pass (OK 4, (OK) 3, no grade 2, bolter 2.5, cut 0) feeding a mini Greenie Board.
