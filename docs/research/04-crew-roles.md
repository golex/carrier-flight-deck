# 04 - Flight Deck Crew Roles by Jersey Color

Scope: Nimitz-class CVN, jet-only cyclic operations (F/A-18E/F, EA-18G). This covers who wears what, where they stand, and what they do during launch, recovery and respot, plus the chain of command, communications and hand signals.

Confidence tags: **[NATOPS]** = NAVAIR 00-80T-120, *CVN Flight/Hangar Deck NATOPS Manual* (15 Dec 2010), quoted or closely paraphrased. **[2+]** = two or more independent secondary sources agree. **[1]** = single secondary source. **[est]** = my estimate for the simulation, not from a source.

---

## 1. Uniform system basics

- A complete flight deck uniform is: flight deck trousers, a **jersey** in the detail's color, a **flotation vest** (float coat or "float coat"/MK1 life vest at night), steel-toe boots, leather gloves, and a **helmet** (the "cranial") with goggles. The billet title is stenciled on the jersey and the vest. **[NATOPS 3.11 / 7.3.2]**
- The jersey, vest and helmet colors **do not always match**. For example, a catapult officer wears a yellow jersey with a green helmet, and an elevator operator wears a blue jersey with a white helmet. See the master table. **[NATOPS App. H]**
- **Pants signal rank:** blue trousers for junior enlisted and petty officers, khaki for chiefs, warrant officers and officers. **[2+]** (Wikipedia, migflug)
- **Helmet markings [NATOPS App. H notes]:**
  - Every helmet has a 6 in square of white reflective tape on the back shell and a 3 x 6 in patch on the front.
  - **Three 1-in international-orange stripes** run fore and aft on top of the tape for: all Air Department officers, Air Department CPOs and LPOs, EOD, ordnance officers and gunners, and the Arresting Gear Topside Petty Officer.
  - A **blue "T"** marks personnel not yet flight-deck qualified (trainees).
  - A **blue "U/I"** marks aircraft directors under instruction.
  - **LSOs are not required to wear helmets** or sound attenuators while controlling aircraft.
- Ordnance jerseys carry a **3-inch black horizontal stripe**. Squadron maintenance jerseys carry a **black stripe** plus the squadron designator. Squadron plane inspectors wear a **black and white checkerboard** pattern. **[NATOPS]**
- New personnel must watch 3 days and 3 nights of flight operations from off the deck, then work 3 days and 3 nights escorted, before they may work the deck unescorted. **[NATOPS 7.3.2]**

### Resolving "troubleshooter"
The word covers three different people:
1. **Squadron plane inspectors / final checkers.** Fleet slang calls them "troubleshooters" or "shooters". They wear a **white jersey with a black-and-white checkerboard pattern and a green helmet**. These are the squadron maintainers who give the last thumbs-up at the catapult. FighterSweep (a pilot's account) calls them troubleshooters in "white checkered flight deck jerseys". NATOPS calls the same people "squadron Plane Checkers" and lists them on its uniform table as "Squadron plane inspector". **[NATOPS + 2]**
2. **GSE troubleshooters.** These are ship or AIMD ground-support-equipment repair people in **green jersey, green helmet, "GSE"**. They have nothing to do with the launch. **[NATOPS]**
3. **Squadron maintenance troubleshooters** who fix jets on the deck between events. They wear **green with a black stripe**, under the "maintenance crews" row. CAGMO supervises "aircraft maintainers, troubleshooters, and plane captains". **[NATOPS 1.7.19]**

For the simulation, model the catapult final checker as **white checkerboard jersey + green helmet**.

## 3. Chain of command and stations

```
Commanding Officer (bridge)
 └─ Air Officer "Air Boss" (CDR, Pri-Fly, top of island, ~6 decks above flight deck)
     ├─ Assistant Air Officer "Mini Boss" (Pri-Fly)
     ├─ Aircraft Handling Officer "Handler" / ACHO (Flight Deck Control, base of island)
     │    ├─ Night Handler (NACHO): respots, elevator traffic, night orders
     │    ├─ Flight Deck Officer (V-1) / Asst FDO / Flight Deck Chief  -> yellow shirts, blue shirts
     │    ├─ Crash & Salvage Officer "Air Bos'n"                        -> red crash & salvage
     │    ├─ Catapult & Arresting Gear Officer (V-2)
     │    │    ├─ Bow Cat Officer / Waist Cat Officer ("Shooters")     -> cat crews (green)
     │    │    └─ Arresting Gear Officer (Recovery Officer)             -> AG crews (green)
     │    ├─ Hangar Deck Officer (V-3)
     │    └─ (coordinates) Aviation Fuels Officer (V-4)                -> purple
     ├─ Air Wing: CAG Maintenance Officer -> squadron coordinators, troubleshooters, plane captains (brown)
     ├─ Air Wing Ordnance Officer "CAG Gunner" / ship's Air Gunner     -> red ordnance
     └─ LSOs (air wing pilots, LSO platform, aft port)
```
**[NATOPS 1.7]**

- **Air Boss (Air Officer):** Usually a Commander and former squadron CO. Responsible for all aircraft operations on the flight and hangar decks and within **5 nm** (the carrier control zone). Chooses the Case for launch and recovery. **[NATOPS + Wikipedia]** One study source says the Air Boss watches landings and activity aft while the **Mini Boss runs launches and deck activity forward**. **[1]**
- **Pri-Fly also holds** the Primary Flight Arresting Gear Controller (sets and checks wire weight settings), the Fresnel lens console operator, and squadron reps (required for Case I and II). **[NATOPS]**
- **Handler (ACHO):** The Air Boss's principal assistant. Works in **Flight Deck Control** at the base of the island at flight deck level, starboard side, with a window onto the deck. Plans and controls every aircraft move on the flight and hangar decks, assigns side numbers to air plan events, and briefs the start and launch sequence, downed and spare aircraft, the tow/taxi-forward plan, the recovery spot and the emergency pull-forward. His main job is avoiding a "**locked deck**". **[NATOPS 7.2.1 + Wikipedia]**
  - **Ouija board:** A tabletop model of the flight and hangar decks at **1/16 in = 1 ft**, with scale aircraft templates. Status is shown with hardware: **bolts upright or on their sides = fueled or not**, colored nuts, bolts and thumbtacks for status (e.g., **purple = needs fuel, green = needs maintenance**). It is run by the handler with junior sailors, who get constant updates from the deck. Electronic versions (ADMACS/eOuija) exist, but the physical board is kept because it works with no power. **[2+]** (Smithsonian Air & Space, Stars and Stripes, Sandboxx, WATM)
  - Also in Flight Deck Control: an air wing maintenance coordinator and squadron reps. A pilot's request to run engines above idle goes plane captain -> air wing coordinator in FDC -> handler. The handler sends a director to check chocks, chains and the area behind the jet, then announces the turn-up on the **5MC**. **[NATOPS 7.4.4.2]**
- **Flight Deck Officer / Flight Deck Chief:** Responsible for safe aircraft movement, security and cleanliness on the flight deck (V-1). Inspects the deck before each evolution: tailpipe clearance, every jet can taxi from its spot, nothing parked over the **safe-park launch lines**. Owns safety in **Fly 2** (amidships). **[NATOPS]** The FDO also gives the Gear Puller "clear deck forward" and similar signals, coordinating with the AGO.
- **Deck zones:** **Fly 1** = bow (cats 1-2), **Fly 2** = amidships and waist, **Fly 3** = landing area aft. The AGO owns safety compliance in Fly 3 and the FDO owns Fly 2. **[NATOPS 5.1.2]** The landing area runs from the fantail to the forward end of the angled deck, including the safe parking area by the island.
- **12-15 yellow shirts** are on the deck during flight operations or a respot, reporting to the handler. **[Wikipedia]** Yellow shirts in the hangar are "hangar rats". On the flight deck they are "bears". **[2+]**
- **Crash and salvage:** A **P-25** mobile firefighting vehicle (750 gal water + 60 gal AFFF) with a 3-person crew (driver, senior man, junior man) is manned during launch, recovery, respot, engine turns, weapons loading and fueling. During recovery a separate **line crew** (crash and salvage supervisor + 2) stands at a predesignated spot. The **crash crane "Tilly"** and forklift are started at the beginning of every recovery and run throughout. A "**hot chocks**" crew (2 crash and salvage crewmen with chocks + a tractor driver with a hot tractor and tow bar) is kept ready for immediate access to the landing area. Without a crash crane, no recoveries are allowed. **[NATOPS 3.4.2.1, 7.4.6; DVIDS]**

## 4. Role-by-role: position and actions in each phase

Positions use the Nimitz deck layout. Cats 1 and 2 are on the bow (Cat 1 starboard, Cat 2 port). Cats 3 and 4 are in the waist on the angle (Cat 3 inboard, Cat 4 outboard/port). The waist cats lie inside the landing area. The landing area is the angled deck, with 4 wires on CVN-68 to -75 (3 on CVN-76/77). The LSO platform is aft port. The island is starboard, with elevators 1 and 2 forward of it, elevator 3 aft of it and elevator 4 on the port side aft of the waist.

### YELLOW: directors and officers

**Aircraft Directors ("plane directors", "bears")**: enlisted ABHs, plus some officers (FDO, Fly-area officers).
- **Launch:**
  - The director takes the jet from the plane captain after the start. Before chocks and chains come off, the director signals "hold brakes", and the pilot acknowledges with a head nod by day or a flashlight at night. The director signals blue shirts and the plane captain to break down the jet.
  - Taxi to the catapult is a **relay**. Each director controls the jet only in his zone, then **passes control** by pointing both arms (or both wands) at the next director. That director must first raise one arm vertically to show he is ready to accept. A director never signals a jet that another director controls, except to prevent an accident. The pilot stops at once if he loses sight of his director. A director tries to stand still while directing. If he must move, he signals "hold brakes", moves, and then resumes. **[NATOPS 7.4.4.3]**
  - **Behind the JBD:** A jet waiting for a loaded catapult is held aft of the raised JBD. A jet with folded wings or an open canopy may not be spotted behind a JBD while the jet on the catapult is at high power. **[NATOPS]**
  - **On the catapult:** The director signals flaps down and wings spread. He stops the jet at the lead-in "wye", where the holdback is fitted. He gives the **disengage steering** signal (forefinger touches the nose, then the arm sweeps down in the direction of movement). On the TSPO's direction he signals **lower launch bar**, then taxis the jet slowly (under 4 mph at the buffer) over the shuttle until the holdback engages. When the TSPO signals tension, the director checks fore and aft, signals **release brakes**, and gives the **tension** signal. Once the TSPO is out from under the jet, he **passes control to the Shooter / Catapult Safety Observer**. **[NATOPS 4.6.1-4.6.2]**
  - If the jet carries forward-firing weapons, the director stops it in the arming area, forward of the JBD with the launch bar not yet over the shuttle. He turns it over to the **ordnance arming safety coordinator** until arming is done. **[NATOPS 4.4.4, 11.1.4]**
- **Recovery:**
  - The **Gear Puller** is the yellow shirt in the landing area. He stands forward of the rollout, slightly to the pilot's view side, and gives the AGO "**clear deck forward**" (one arm swept from overhead to horizontal and back, with an amber wand at night) or "**foul deck**" (arms crossed overhead, fists clenched). **[NATOPS 5.2.2]**
  - After the trap, the Gear Puller does not enter until the jet stops. On the hook runner's "wire clear", he signals **hook up**, then **hold brakes**, then **off brakes**, and taxis the jet out of the landing area. On the way he gives **fold wings** (and flaps up) and passes control to the next director. **[NATOPS 5.2.3, 7.4.6]**
  - A string of directors then brings the jet to the **de-arm area** if needed, and then to its parking spot. That spot is usually the bow or forward "row" during cyclic ops, or the Fly 2 / "junkyard" areas, per the handler's recovery spot.
- **Parking and respot:** The director picks a final position where he can see the tightest clearance. He posts wing and tail safeties whenever the jet passes within 5 ft of an obstruction. He calls brakes (a sharp whistle blast plus the visual signal), calls chocks, and stays with the jet until the initial tiedowns are on. Directors carry **whistles held in the mouth** during every move. **[NATOPS 7.4.3, 7.4.7]**
- **Count:** 12-15 on deck at once. **[Wikipedia]** About 1 per taxi "leg" plus 1-2 per catapult and 1 Gear Puller. **[est]**

**Catapult Officer ("Shooter", Launching Officer)**: commissioned officer, **yellow jersey, green helmet**.
- **Station:** On the deck beside the catapult in deckedge mode, or inside the **ICCS "bubble"**, a retractable glass-topped control station flush with the deck. One bubble sits between Cats 1 and 2 and one between Cats 3 and 4. **[NATOPS; ICCS positions from deck plans]**
- **Launch:**
  - He confirms the CSV (steam) setting from type, weight and wind, and checks the wind and the **green rotating beacon**.
  - In deckedge mode he takes control from the director, then gives **FINAL TURNUP**: a raised hand rotated vigorously over the head, or a green wand circled at night. For afterburner shots he holds both arms overhead (the CRT signal).
  - He receives the pilot's **salute**, or the navigation lights coming on at night. He returns the salute, scans the jet, the deck forward, the checkers' thumbs and the bow/angle safety, and times the deck pitch.
  - He then gives the **fire signal**: he sweeps his raised hand down in the direction of launch, **touches the deck**, and returns his hand to horizontal pointing down the track.
  - In ICCS mode the Catapult Safety Observer does the topside part (crouches and gives a thumbs-up), and the Shooter presses FIRE. **[NATOPS 4.5.3, 4.6.3]**
- **Recovery:** Not active. Waist cat crews make the waist safe for landing: shuttles aft, JBDs flush, ICCS lowered, slot buttons in. **[NATOPS 5.2.1]**

**Arresting Gear Officer (AGO, "Recovery Officer")**: commissioned officer, **yellow jersey, green helmet**.
- **Station:** "On deck" means the starboard catwalk or the flight deck edge abeam the wires, where he can see the whole landing area. He holds the deck status light "**pickle**" switch.
- **Recovery:**
  - He identifies the type of the approaching jet and verifies that all engines are set, e.g., "ALL ENGINES SET THREE SIX ZERO, HORNET".
  - He gets "clear deck forward" from the Gear Puller and checks that the pendants are taut and the sheaves are up.
  - He then switches the deck status light **RED -> GREEN** ("clear deck"). The LSO talker relays this as "GEAR SET, THREE SIX ZERO, HORNET, CLEAR DECK".
  - At touchdown he switches **GREEN -> RED** (foul).
  - After every trap, bolter, touch-and-go or wave-off the deck stays RED until the checks are repeated. **[NATOPS 5.2.2-5.2.3]**
  - He clears people to cross the landing area. He points at the person, then swings his arm to the far side (a red wand at night). The person crosses at least 10 ft aft of the No. 1 wire. **[NATOPS 5.1.2]**

**Handler / Flight Deck Officer / Flight Deck Chief / Tractor King:** See section 3. The **Tractor King** (yellow jersey, blue vest, "TK") runs the tractor fleet. **[NATOPS]**

### GREEN: catapult, arresting gear, maintenance and support

**Catapult crew (V-2, "C" on the jersey)**, per catapult. Topside **[NATOPS]**:

| Billet | Uniform | Position and duties |
|---|---|---|
| Catapult Safety Observer (CSO) | Green jersey, yellow vest | Shooter's representative topside (ICCS ships). Checks the deck forward, JBD up, configuration, arming, green beacon, TSPO thumbs-up. Gives TSPO the **hook-up** signal. After the pilot's salute, crouches all nearby crew, scans, then **drops to one knee with a thumbs-up** (vertical green wand at night) to the Shooter. |
| Topside Safety Petty Officer (TSPO, "hookup petty officer") | Green / "A" or "C" | Under the nose gear. Checks the holdback (repeatable-release holdback reset indications) and that the launch bar is seated in the shuttle spreader. Gives the **tension** signal by sweeping one arm with the forefinger pointing down the track (white stubby wand at night). **Last man out from under the aircraft.** Thumbs-up or steady white stubby when clear. |
| Holdback man | Green | Fits the holdback bar and tension bar to the nose gear while the jet is stopped at the wye, before the NGL approach ramp. |
| Centerdeck operator | Green | On waist ops, near the cats and the anemometer. Thumbs-up if the wind is within limits. Relays type, weight, side number and CSV to the bubble. Keeps a spare set of Shooter's wands. |
| Deckedge operator | Green | **Starboard catwalk** beside the bow cats (deckedge mode). Presses MIL PWR / FINAL READY / FIRE. Holds **both arms overhead, hands open**, until he lowers them to press FIRE or SUSPEND. Retracts the shuttle after each shot. |
| JBD operator / safety | Green-white vest "JBD Safety" | Raises the JBD only once the jet's tail is clear of the arc, lowers it after the shot. |
| Weight board operator | Green | Shows the pilot the computed gross weight on a board. The pilot confirms with a thumbs-up, or a circled flashlight at night. To disagree: palm up moved upward means raise, palm down moved horizontally means lower. **[NATOPS 4.4.2]** |
| Bow/Angle safety observer | Green | At the bow or the angle edge. Shows green (thumbs-up/green light) when the launch path and pattern are clear. |
| Below deck (not visible) | - | CCP operator, monitor, water brake, retraction engine, steam/launch valve operators. |

- **Count per catapult:** about 8-10 green topside plus 4-6 below deck. **[est from billet list]**
- **Launch timing:** Shuttles may sit in battery no more than 5 min. Night/Case III launches are spaced at least **30 s** apart. The bow and waist are never fired simultaneously. A 4 s delay normally follows the control wipeout before the F/A-18 pilot salutes. **[NATOPS 4.4.1, 4.7, 4.2.4]**

**Arresting gear crew (V-2, "A")**
- **Hook runner** (green helmet, green jersey, "A"; 1 red standard wand at night):
  - Stands at the edge of the landing area just outside the foul line, abeam the wires on the starboard side.
  - After the jet has **completely stopped** and rolled back a few feet so the pendant drops, he runs in.
  - He confirms the hook and jet are clear of the cross-deck pendant and gives "**WIRE CLEAR**" to the Gear Puller (vertical arm motion, or red wand pointed at the Gear Puller).
  - If the wire is hung, he signals HOOK DOWN, then PULL BACK. The deckedge operator retracts slightly to make slack, then he signals HOOK UP.
  - Once the hook is up and well clear, he signals the deckedge operator to "**RETRACT**" and confirms the signal was received before leaving.
  - Some carry a pole to flick the wire off. **[NATOPS 5.2.3; pole: forum, 1]**
- **AG deckedge operator:** Starboard catwalk. Calls "**GROOVE**" as the jet reaches short final and "**RAMP**" as it crosses the fantail over the sound-powered circuit. Retracts the purchase cable only on a positive signal. **[NATOPS]**
- **AG Topside Petty Officer** (orange-striped helmet): runs the topside crew, spare pendants (4 CDPs on deck for CVN-68 to -75, 2 uncrated) and tools, the barricade hatch and tractor. **[NATOPS]**
- **Wire supports / gear checkers / safeties:** A/G deck checker (white stubby), A/G safety and VLA safety (red wands). If the crew must enter the landing area during a recovery, a **safety man faces aft with arms crossed overhead** forward of them. **[NATOPS 5.1.2]**
- **Below deck:** 1 engine operator per arresting engine (4 on CVN-68 to -75) and sheave damper operators. All repeat "GROOVE, RAMP". Anyone not ready calls "**FOUL DECK, FOUL DECK**". **[NATOPS]**
- **Count:** about 6-8 topside green plus 1 AGO plus 1 Gear Puller. **[est]**

**Squadron maintenance (green with black stripe):** Troubleshooters and specialists (avionics, hydraulics, engines) stand by in the squadron line area or at the catapult. They fix "downs" during start-up, help the plane captain on recovery (post-flight checks) and do maintenance between events. **[NATOPS 1.7.19; DCS guide]**

**Others in green:** GSE troubleshooters ("GSE"), photographers / Mass Communication Specialists ("P"), helicopter LSE ("H", red helmet), cargo handlers (white helmet, "Supply"/"POSTAL"), VLA (lens) electricians. **[NATOPS; Wikipedia]**

### BLUE: plane handlers (V-1/V-3)
- **Plane handlers / chock-and-chain men** (blue/blue, crew number):
  - On the director's signal they remove tiedown chains (jointly with the plane captain) and **chocks** (chocks are the plane handler's job).
  - A tow needs a **director, tractor driver, plane captain (brake rider), 2 plane handlers (chock walkers), and 2 wing safeties + 1 tail safety** when within 5 ft of an obstruction. **[NATOPS 7.4.3]**
  - Chock walkers walk **abreast the main wheel on the outboard side**, never in its path, and carry no chains while walking chocks.
  - On recovery they **stand by in designated areas** and put on the **initial tiedowns**. Initial tiedowns are 4-point or 6-point. The plane captain then adds chains up to the intermediate requirement. **[NATOPS 7.4.6, 8.7.3]**
  - They approach jets with turning engines from the side and crouch by the wheels.
- **Tractor drivers** (blue/blue, "Tractor"): Move aircraft only under a director's positive control. They stop if a signal is unclear, never ride passengers, and move no faster than handlers can walk. Tow bars stay attached to all deck aircraft during a launch unless the handler directs otherwise, so an emergency **respot forward** can happen fast. **[NATOPS 7.4.3, 7.4.5]**
- **Elevator operators** (white helmet, blue jersey, "E"): Each elevator has **1 director + 1 operator** on the flight deck, the same at hangar level, plus 1 pump-room operator. Before every cycle the warning horn sounds **3 times**, and it sounds continuously while the elevator moves. Stanchions are raised first. Riders stand on the inboard half. **[NATOPS 7.5]**
- **Messengers / phone talkers** (white helmet, blue jersey, "T"): sound-powered phone talkers for Flight Deck Control and the elevators.
- Blue shirts are the most numerous crew on the deck. Most ABHs start in blue and move up to yellow. **[NHHC infographic; 2+]**
- **Count:** about 30-60 on deck. **[est]**

### RED: ordnance, crash and salvage, EOD
- **Squadron ordnancemen** ("ordies"; red/red, 3-in black stripe):
  - They load and download weapons on the deck between events. Mechanical latching must finish **before engine start**.
  - The **arming crew** ("CA" in white reflective; supervisor with a red banded wand) stands at the arming area just aft of each catapult shuttle and forward of the JBD.
  - The director hands the jet to the arming safety coordinator. The ordie shows "**hands up**" so both pilots' hands are visible away from the switches, arms the weapons and pulls the safety pins, then shows the pins to the pilot and hands back control.
  - Arming happens only with the jet stopped, forward of the raised JBD, flaps set, the **launch bar resting on top of the shuttle**, and nobody in the weapon's path. **[NATOPS 11.1.4; FighterSweep]**
- **De-arm crew:** Works right after the jet taxis clear of the landing area, at the de-arm area, before it goes to the recovery spot. The jet must have a clear area ahead if it carries forward-firing weapons. **[NATOPS 11.1.7]**
- **Crash and salvage** (red/red "Crash/Salvage"; ABHs): P-25 crew, hot-chocks crew, line crew at recoveries, silver proximity suits. They fight the fire, rescue the aircrew, then "overhaul". The Tilly lifts the wreck clear of the landing area. **[NATOPS; DVIDS]**
- **EOD** (red/red, "EOD" in black, orange-striped helmet): hung ordnance and emergencies.
- **Ordnance QA / safety:** white jersey, red vest.

### BROWN: plane captains (squadron)
- **Plane captain** (brown/brown, squadron designator): Each "owns" one aircraft, works out of the squadron line shack, and is responsible for its cleanliness, servicing (fluids, tires, LOX check), FOD checks and pre/post-flight inspections. **[DVIDS; 2+]**
  - **Pre-launch:** Stands at the nose of his jet (left side, in view of the cockpit). He helps the pilot strap in, then runs the start with hand signals: APU/engine start, flight control checks, "wipe-out", trim, flaps, and fluid-leak checks. The plane captain has control **for preflight checks only**. He cannot have chains removed or wings spread without a director's clearance. He removes the tow bar before start. He passes the jet to the yellow shirt and salutes the pilot. **[NATOPS 7.4.4.2; DCS]**
  - **Recovery:** Meets his jet at its parking spot. He helps chock and chain it, adds tiedowns, installs gear downlocks and intake/exhaust covers, gets the pilot's up/down gripe, and checks that fuel (purple) reaches the jet. He is also the "**brake rider**" in the cockpit during tows and respots. **[NATOPS 7.4.3, 8.5.3]**
  - **Count:** 1 per aircraft that is turning or moving, so about 15-20 during a cycle. **[est]**
- **Line LPO / Line CPO:** brown jersey, green helmet. **QA LPO:** white jersey, brown helmet.

### PURPLE: aviation fuels ("grapes", V-4)
- **Crew:** At least 1 crew leader on the **4JG sound-powered circuit** at the deck fueling station plus 1 nozzle operator per hose. A qualified fuels flight-deck supervisor acts as safety PO. **[NATOPS 8.7.1]**
- **Recovery:** The crew stands by at the deck-edge fueling stations before the recovery. Jets are fueled as soon as possible after they park. The crew grounds the jet, connects single-point pressure refuel and reports to the AVFUELS control talker.
- **Hot refuel:** F/A-18 family jets may be **hot-refueled with both engines running**, the only type allowed to. The jet is chocked with 4- or 6-point tiedowns, and the canopy stays closed. **[NATOPS 8.7.3]**
- **Count:** about 10-20 on deck during a respot. **[est]**

### WHITE: safety, inspection, LSO, medical and others
- **Squadron plane inspector / final checker ("troubleshooter")** (white checkerboard, green helmet; blue stubby wand at night):
  - Walks with his jet as it taxis to the catapult. He sweeps the radome, probes, gear doors, wings (wing-lock confirmation) and tanks.
  - On the catapult he stands where he can see the nozzles and controls while the jet runs up. He watches the control wipe-out, the afterburner light-off and nozzle action, and looks for leaks.
  - He gives a **thumbs-up held high** (steady blue stubby at night), or SUSPEND. **No launch without it.**
  - Typically 1-2 per jet, from the jet's own squadron. **[NATOPS 4.5.3/4.6.3; FighterSweep]**
- **Landing Signal Officers** (white vest "LSO", no helmet):
  - On the **LSO platform, aft port side**, with **no more than 6 people** (Controlling LSO, backup LSO, Paddles' talker/spotter, writer, etc.).
  - They control lens brightness, cut and wave-off lights, and the final approach by radio ("Roger ball"). They grade every pass.
  - The LSO talker relays deck status, e.g., "HORNET, ALL DOWN"... "CLEAR DECK".
  - Wave-off lights stay on whenever the lens is lit and no LSO is on the platform. Without a "clear deck" the LSO waves off at **1/2 nm or more**. **[NATOPS 1.7.24, 5.1.2]**
- **Safety observers** ("SAFETY"), **ALRE QA**, **Ordnance QA**, **LOX crew** ("LOX"), **medical** (red cross), **Air Transfer Officer** ("TRANSFER OFFICER", passengers, mail and cargo from C-2/CMV-22), **IW** (security), VIPs and media escorts.

## 5. Communications

- **Hand signals (NAVAIR 00-80T-113, Aircraft Signals NATOPS).** Signals to the pilot are given **above the waist and shoulders**. Signals to ground crew are given **at or below the waist**. **[1, Brainscape]** **Emergency stop** is mandatory. All other director signals are advisory. **[2+]**
- **No voice while in tension.** The voice circuit stays silent from "taking tension" until launch, suspend or hangfire. **[NATOPS]**
- **SUSPEND:** Anyone topside can stop a launch with **crossed arms overhead** by day or a **horizontal wand movement** at night. The deckedge operator signals suspend with crossed arms or a **red wand vertical overhead**. The pilot can suspend by radio ("SUSPEND, SUSPEND CATAPULT NO. __") and by shaking his head, or at night by not turning on his navigation lights. After a suspend: untension, shuttle forward of the launch bar, and only then the THROTTLE BACK signal. **[NATOPS 4.11]**
- **Sound-powered phones and deck circuits:** the "mouse ears" cranial has phone-talker headsets. Circuits include 4JG (fuels), the arresting gear circuit (GROOVE/RAMP/engine-set reports) and catapult crew circuits. **[NATOPS]**
- **Announcing systems:**
  - **5MC** is the flight deck announcing system.
  - **1MC** is shipwide and is used for flight quarters ("Flight quarters, flight quarters, all hands man your flight quarters stations" is the typical form).
  - **3MC** is the hangar deck.
  - There is a flight warning alarm, and a **crash alarm** for an actual on-deck mishap. **[NATOPS 3.4]**
- **Air Boss calls (NATOPS-documented):**
  - "MAN ALL RECOVERY STATIONS" on the 5MC, 15 minutes before recovery.
  - "PADDLES/TOWER, LENS ON, YOU HAVE CONTROL".
  - "LAND AIRCRAFT" on the 5MC.
  - "ROGER, GEAR... LENS SET... THREE SIX ZERO HORNET".
  - Announcements of hung ordnance on approaching aircraft and of engine turn-ups.
  - "Clear to launch spot five" (helicopters). **[navy.mil]**
  - Common real-world (unofficial) 5MC lines heard in videos: "Check chinstraps, sleeves down, goggles down... start 'em up", "Aircrew, man your aircraft". **[unverified, mark as flavor]**
- **Rotating beacons on the island:** **RED** means the deck is closed (no launch or land). **Green** means the deck is open (launch clearance for the cat officer, or "land aircraft" aft). The Air Boss changes the beacon from **red/amber to green** to clear launches. The fore and aft beacons are separate. **[NATOPS 4.5.3, 5.2.1]**
- **Deck status light (AGO pickle, shown to the LSO):** GREEN = clear deck, RED = foul deck. The AGO goes red at each touchdown. **[NATOPS]**
- **Catapult deck lights:** green = military power, amber = combat power (afterburner), both flashing = final ready. The deckedge has a red suspend light. **[NATOPS 4.6.3]**
- **Whistles:** Directors and handling crews hold whistles in their mouths during moves. A sharp blast means brakes. **[NATOPS 7.4.3]**

## 7. Phase-by-phase choreography summary (who moves when)

**A. Pre-launch (about 45 min out): man up and start.**
1. Aircrew walk to their jets (brown shirts waiting, ordies finishing loading, grapes finishing fuel, troubleshooters on deck).
2. About 30 min out, the Air Boss calls for engine starts. Each jet has a plane captain at the nose and a yellow shirt monitoring. A crash and salvage P-25 is manned. **[Wikipedia; NATOPS]**
3. The plane captain runs the start and checks, then the jet is passed to a director. **[NATOPS 7.4.4.2]**

**B. Taxi (about 15 min out).**
1. The director signals hold brakes. Blue shirts and the plane captain pull the chains, then the blue shirts pull the chocks.
2. The jet moves on a relay of 3-5 directors to a cat. Wings stay folded until near the catapult, and the jet holds behind the JBD.
3. The final checker walks alongside the jet. **[NATOPS]**

**C. On catapult (about 60-90 s per jet) [est from steps].**
1. Weight board shown, weight confirmed. Wings spread and locked, flaps down.
2. Stop at the wye. Disengage steering. Holdback fitted. Launch bar down.
3. The JBD goes up once the tail is clear. Arming happens if needed.
4. Taxi over the shuttle into the buffer.
5. Hook-up signal from the Shooter to the CSO to the TSPO. TSPO tension signal, then director off brakes + tension, then tension is taken.
6. The TSPO checks the launch bar seating, gets out from under the jet and gives a thumbs-up. The director passes control to the Shooter/CSO.
7. Run-up at MIL (or afterburner). Wipe-out. Checkers give thumbs-up. The pilot waits about 4 s, then salutes.
8. The CSO kneels with a thumbs-up. The Shooter scans, then touches the deck (or presses FIRE). The catapult fires: about 2 s to roughly 150 kt.
9. The shuttle retracts, the JBD drops and the next jet rolls forward.

**D. Recovery (deck goes from "launch" to "land" in minutes).**
1. 15-min "MAN ALL RECOVERY STATIONS". Waist cats are made safe. The LSO mans the platform.
2. The AGO and Gear Puller check clear deck, then "LAND AIRCRAFT" is called and the beacon goes green aft.
3. For each pass: engines set ("ONE SET THREE SIX ZERO..."), "GROOVE", "RAMP", touchdown, light to RED.
4. The jet rolls back, then the hook runner and Gear Puller run in. Wire clear, hook up, brakes, off brakes. The jet taxis clear, with fold wings on the way. The hook runner signals retract and the wire returns to battery.
5. The AGO checks again, then clear deck (GREEN) for the next jet, ideally **45-60 s** between traps. **[Wikipedia; NATOPS]**
6. The recovered jet goes through the de-arm area (red shirts), then a director relay to its spot. Blue shirts chock and put on initial chains. The plane captain adds chains and downlocks. The pilot shuts down only on the director's signal. Grapes refuel.

**E. Respot (between and after events).**
- "Flight Quarters for Respot" is a separate condition. **[NATOPS 3.4.1.2]**
- The handler plans it on the Ouija board. Directors and tractor teams (director + tractor driver + plane captain brake rider + 2 chock walkers + wing and tail safeties as needed) tow jets from the bow and landing-area recovery spots back to start spots aft and amidships for the next launch. Some jets go down to the hangar on elevators (1 director + 1 operator per level).
- During the respot: fueling (purple), re-arming (red), maintenance (green), and plane captain turnaround inspections (brown).
- Tow bars stay on all deck aircraft for an emergency "**respot forward**" (pull-forward) to clear the landing area for a bolter-heavy or emergency recovery. **[NATOPS 7.4.3-7.4.5]**
- At the end of the day, jets are parked on the bow to keep the landing area clear, then respotted at night for the first launch (run by the NACHO). **[Wikipedia; NATOPS 1.7.6]**

## Simulation implications

1. **Crew entity schema:** `{role, jerseyColor, helmetColor, vestColor, marking, nightWands[]}`. Use the NATOPS App. H table. Key mismatches to render: Shooter/AGO (yellow jersey + green helmet), CSO (green jersey + yellow vest), elevator operator and phone talker (blue jersey + white helmet), final checker (white checkerboard + green helmet), helicopter LSE (green jersey + red helmet), LSO (no helmet), and the 3-in black stripe on ordnance jerseys. A top-down view mainly shows the **helmet and shoulder color**, so draw the jersey as the body and the helmet as a dot.
2. **Seven primary colors** for the legend. Suggested hues: yellow #F2C811, green #2E8B3A, blue #1F5FBF, red #C62828, brown #6D4C2F, purple #6A3FA0, white #F5F5F5 (with a black checker variant).
3. **Director relay:** Each taxiing jet is controlled by exactly one director at a time. Handoff animation: the receiving director raises an arm, the current director points both arms at him, and control transfers. A jet stops if no director is ready (this is a natural source of queues).
4. **Catapult micro-sequence state machine** (per jet, about 60-90 s): weight board → wings spread → stop at wye / holdback → launch bar down → JBD up → (arming) → taxi into buffer → hook-up → tension → TSPO exits → control to shooter → run-up → checker thumbs-up → salute → CSO kneel → touch deck → fire (2 s stroke) → shuttle retract → JBD down. Night launches need ≥30 s spacing. Never fire bow and waist at the same moment.
5. **Recovery micro-sequence** (per trap, 45-60 s): deck status light green → GROOVE → RAMP → touchdown → light red → rollout and rollback → hook runner + Gear Puller enter → wire clear → hook up → taxi out with wings folding → retract → AGO checks → green. If the light is not green when the jet is at about 1/2 nm, it is waved off.
6. **People positions to model:** Shooter in the bubble or at the deck edge; CSO kneeling beside the nose; TSPO under the nose; checkers off the wingtips; JBD safety at the JBD; deckedge operator in the starboard catwalk; hook runner(s) at the starboard edge abeam the wires; AGO at the starboard edge; Gear Puller forward on the angle; LSOs on the aft port platform; P-25 parked near the island; hot-chocks crew near the landing area; plane captains at the nose of their parked jets.
7. **Respot teams:** director + tractor + brake rider (brown) + 2 chock walkers (blue) + wing and tail safeties when within 5 ft. Speed no faster than walking (about 3 mph). Elevator cycles: 3 horn blasts, then the elevator moves.
8. **Status lights to visualize:** island rotating beacons (red, amber, green, fore and aft separately), the AGO deck status light (red/green), catapult power lights (green MIL, amber AB, flashing = final ready), and wave-off lights.
9. **Audio cues** (TTS candidates): "Man all recovery stations", "Land aircraft", "Groove... Ramp", "Clear deck" / "Foul deck, foul deck", "Roger, gear, lens set, three six zero Hornet", "Suspend, suspend catapult two", whistle blasts for brakes.
10. **Night mode:** Replace arm signals with colored wand glows: amber pairs (directors), red/green (shooters, AGO), blue stubby (checkers), white stubby (TSPO), red (hook runner, ordnance banded).
11. **Ouija board inset:** A mini-map of the deck mirroring jet positions, with colored pins (purple = needs fuel, green = needs maintenance, red = ordnance) to show the handler's view. This is a good "complexity" teaching device.
12. **Counts:** Show about 150-250 people topside at peak, with blue the most numerous, about 12-15 yellow, about 20-30 green at the cats, and 1 brown per active jet.
